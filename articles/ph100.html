From PUP Sta. Mesa to the PH100

November 2026 · StellarPH PH100 · Carlos Jerico Dela Torre, Aidan Tiu, Rhandie Sales Jr., and Gerald Berongoy

Building software while helping run student tech communities at the Polytechnic University of the Philippines meant learning to work with limited time and resources. For us, that usually meant moving between campus responsibilities during the day and engineering work at night.

Throughout 2025 and into 2026, the four of us, Carlos Jerico Dela Torre, Aidan Tiu, Rhandie Sales Jr., and Gerald Berongoy, split our time between two commitments: helping student builders find opportunities to learn and work together, and building software that could serve real users. We did this before Axon Enjin was founded in February 2026 and continued after the studio began taking shape.

During the day, Carlos and Rhandie helped manage student developer programs, review workshop curricula, and coordinate activities for student builders across Google Developer Groups (GDG) on Campus PUP, the Microsoft Student Community (MSC), and Seekers Guild. In the evenings, the four of us would return to our own projects. We debugged database migrations, reduced scope ahead of hackathon deadlines, and worked on production systems for clients, often from the same table with our laptops open and Discord running in the background.

The two commitments competed for time. An afternoon spent organizing a campus tech talk was time we could not spend on a project sprint. A late deployment could leave us heading into class or a community meeting with very little sleep. People sometimes advised us to choose one path: focus on commercial work and leave the community responsibilities behind, or pause the studio until after graduation. Neither option felt right. The more useful question was how to make the two reinforce each other without compromising the quality of our work.

Learning to Carry the Work Together

We began to see community leadership and studio execution as connected experiences. The responsibilities were different, but many of the skills carried across: setting standards, mentoring people, communicating clearly, and keeping a team moving when time was limited.

Rhandie brought that experience into engineering. At the PUP Microsoft Student Community, he served as Director for Web Development before becoming Vice President. The role involved setting development standards, mentoring junior developers, and keeping internal tools working within the constraints of a student organization. At Axon Enjin, he applied that same discipline as Co-Founder and Full-Stack Engineer, working on applications designed to handle real users without making the architecture more complicated than it needed to be.

Carlos approached the overlap through product strategy and ecosystem building. Alongside his role as Co-Founder and CEO of Axon Enjin, he co-founded and led Seekers Guild, served as CTO and Technology Executive Consultant at GDG on Campus PUP, and advised Microsoft Student Community Philippines and MSC PUP as a Technology Executive Consultant. He also worked as AI Product & Strategy Manager at Eden Ventures. Working across product development and student communities gave him a broader view of how to identify a problem, define a useful solution, and explain technical decisions to people with different levels of experience.

That arrangement only worked because the studio was not dependent on one or two people to keep every project moving. When Carlos and Rhandie needed to attend campus meetings or handle community responsibilities, Aidan Tiu and Gerald Berongoy continued work in the codebase. They kept deployments moving, resolved production issues, and took features through to completion. The four of us had different responsibilities, but we relied on each other to keep the work moving.

Across 2025 and 2026, that shared effort contributed to projects including Archon, Mate, Axial, and the Full Cup Café website. It also built on our experience supporting student developer organizations and campus technology, including GDG PUP NEXUS. These projects did not come from a single uninterrupted sprint. They were the result of a team learning to hand work over, communicate decisions, and keep commitments visible.

Recognition, and the People Behind It

In October 2026, StellarPH (stellarph.io), a Philippine startup enabler and community builder headquartered in Cebu, published the 2026 StellarPH PH100. The annual list recognizes what StellarPH describes as the 100 brightest minds in the Philippines under 30, spanning areas such as startups, engineering, product, and community leadership.

Two of the names on the roster were Axon Enjin co-founders and PUP builders Carlos Jerico Dela Torre and Rhandie Sales Jr.

At the StellarPH PH100 Celebration Night on November 6, 2026, they joined founders, software engineers, product managers, and community builders from across the country. Seeing two people from a state university in Sta. Mesa included in a national recognition list was meaningful to the team. Their work had grown through both sides of the same experience: building software at Axon Enjin and helping develop the student communities that gave them an early place to learn and contribute.

The announcement carried two individual names, but neither of us got there alone. Our projects depended on people who reviewed code, tested features, mentored teammates, organized workshops, and stepped in when something went wrong. The recognition belongs in part to the communities that gave us room to learn and people to build with.

At Seekers Guild and Axon Enjin, we often return to the same idea: You will do very well on your own, but with others, you can be magnificent. The PH100 recognition is a reminder of what can happen when people build together, even without the resources or connections often associated with the technology industry. We are proud of the recognition, but even more grateful for the teams and communities that made the work possible.
