Shipping for our own campus

July 2026 · PUPreneurship Philanthropic Institution Award · Carlos Jerico Dela Torre, Aidan Tiu, Rhandie Sales Jr., and Gerald Berongoy

On the third floor of the University Library, inside the NALLRC at the Polytechnic University of the Philippines (PUP) Mabini Campus in Sta. Mesa, Manila, four PUP College of Business Administration alumni built Full Cup Café. Harish Ananda Athauda, Justine Mae Miralles, Mark Jason Pineda, and Theekshana Claire Athauda wanted to create more than another place to buy coffee. They designed the café as a calmer space where students and faculty could take a break from the pressure of campus life.

A physical business has immediate expenses. Rent, equipment, supplies, staffing, and product quality all need attention, especially during the early months. A custom website may be useful, but it can be difficult to justify when every peso has a more urgent operational purpose. For young businesses, the practical choice often becomes relying entirely on social media or paying for a website service that adds another recurring expense.

When an opportunity came up in mid-2026 for Axon Enjin to become Full Cup Café's pro-bono digital partner, we saw a chance to support a business growing in the same university community that had helped shape our own work. Axon Enjin had been founded in February that year, and our schedule was already split between commercial deadlines, coursework, and national hackathons. Taking on a pro-bono project meant setting aside real engineering time during an already busy period.

We did not want the fact that the work was unpaid to become an excuse for lower standards. If our name appeared in the footer of fullcupcafe.com as its digital partner, the site had to be reliable, fast, and maintainable. The café needed a useful platform, not a template that would become another problem for its founders to manage.

Building a Website Without Building a New Expense

We approached the project as both an engineering and a cost problem. A website is not much of a gift if the business is left with recurring infrastructure costs it cannot comfortably support once the developers step away.

For fullcupcafe.com, we used the Next.js and edge deployment workflow already established at Axon Enjin. Our repository structure, deployment hooks, and build checks were standardized, which reduced the setup work required for a new project. Each pull request triggered an automated preview deployment. This allowed us to share updates with the Full Cup Café founders and review layout or copy changes without requiring everyone to be available at the same time.

The other challenge was translating the character of the physical café into a website. Full Cup Café was meant to feel calm and welcoming, so the site needed to communicate that identity without becoming slow on a campus mobile connection. We kept the interface focused, used clean typography, optimized the images, and relied on static edge caching. We avoided adding runtime dependencies that did not improve the experience for visitors.

Using the workflow we already had in place, we designed, built, and deployed the site in under a week. In the Lighthouse tests we ran, the live build recorded a performance score above 95. The edge-based setup also kept recurring hosting costs close to zero for the café, rather than leaving its founders with a substantial monthly infrastructure bill.

Recognition at PUP

On July 29, 2026, the PUP College of Business Administration recognized Axon Enjin with the PUPreneurship Philanthropic Institution Award for its contribution to empowering young university entrepreneurs.

The recognition meant a great deal to us because the work was close to home. Five months after founding Axon Enjin, we were back at our university receiving an award for supporting entrepreneurs from the same community where we had learned to build. Just a short walk from the awarding venue, Full Cup Café was serving students and faculty with its website already live.

The Full Cup Café project was one tangible example of the kind of support we wanted to provide. The award was broader recognition of Axon Enjin's contribution to young entrepreneurs, rather than a prize for a single website. Still, the project captured an idea we want to keep applying: when a small team has a repeatable engineering process, it can deliver useful digital infrastructure without passing unnecessary costs on to the people it is trying to help.

For us, pro-bono work is not about lowering the standard because there is no invoice. It is about using the capabilities and systems we already have to make a project more affordable for someone who needs them. Investing in a good deployment pipeline, reusable components, and careful infrastructure choices lowers the effort required for each new build. That makes it easier to support a local business while still treating the work with the same care as a commercial engagement.

The award was a good day for the team. The more concrete result is still on the third floor of the library: a local café with a working website and fewer infrastructure costs to carry.
